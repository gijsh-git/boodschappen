# Aanbiedingen

> Deel van de systeembeschrijving; de index staat in `CLAUDE.md`. Een verwijzing als (see "Offers") gaat naar een kopje in dit bestand of in een ander bestand in `docs/systeem/`; de index zegt welk. Werk dit bestand bij in dezelfde commit als de wijziging die het gedrag verandert.

## Offers

`articles` (primary key `supermarket` + `article_id`, plus `webshop_id`, `title`, `brand`, `size`, `category`) holds every article ever seen in an offer and is never cleaned up: `article_id` means the same as `purchases.article_id` (for AH the `hqId`), so the table is also the translation from a receipt's article number to an article. `offers` (unique on `supermarket`, `offer_id`, `valid_from`) holds title, `discount_text`, `labels` (jsonb as the source gives them: code plus numbers), `discount_type` (code of the first label), `valid_from`/`valid_to`, `is_group` and `store_only`; `offer_articles` links the two and carries `price` and `bonus_price` of that week. Authenticated users can only select. Writing goes through `save_offers(p_supermarket, p_offers)`, executable by the service role only: it upserts articles and offers, replaces the articles of an offer (unless the offer has `complete: false`, a group whose articles could not be fetched), skips articles without an `article_id`, and deletes offers that expired more than 28 days ago (`c_bewaren`). The service role key never leaves Supabase: the Edge Function `aanbiedingen-opslaan` (deployed without JWT verification) compares the header `x-aanbiedingen-sleutel` in constant time with the secret `AANBIEDINGEN_SLEUTEL`, refuses bodies over 5 MB and then calls only `save_offers` with the service role from its own environment, returning its result. `scripts/ah-bonus-opslaan.py` (dry run without `--echt`) maps `data/ah-bonus.json` to that format and posts `{ supermarkt, aanbiedingen }` to the function with `AANBIEDINGEN_SLEUTEL` from the environment. `.github/workflows/ah-bonus.yml` runs `scripts/ah-bonus` and then that script every Monday and Thursday morning, with `AANBIEDINGEN_SLEUTEL` as a repository secret (the repo is public, so the service role key must never be stored there); GitHub switches off a schedule after 60 days without a commit in the repo. The app reads them only through the matching RPCs below.

## Bonus label

The "Bonus" label on the list comes from the offers (the functions are still called `loadDeals()` and `Logica.laadDeals()`): `loadDeals()` calls `Logica.laadDeals()`, which keeps the result per item (`Logica.dealsVan()`) and calls `offers_for_list(p_list)` (see "Matching offers"), returning `(item_id, supermarkt, aantal, weggeklikt, korting, geldig_tot)`. `loadDeals()` runs after `loadItems()`, after your own insert and on a realtime item INSERT; `itemRow()` shows a "Bonus" label with the stores below it (`Logica.dealWinkels()`, full names from `WINKELS`), and `renderBonus()` shows in the header how many products on the list have an offer. `korting` and `geldig_tot` are only filled when the offer itself is on the list (`items.offer_id`, from Voor jou): the label then shows the discount ("2 voor 3.99") with "t/m zo" below it instead of "Bonus" and the store (`Logica.eigenAanbieding()`), the same look as a chosen offer. For an item that has offers through its name the label stays "Bonus", because there can be several; the amount saved is not returned.

## Matching offers

Roadmap step 5, part 1. All matching is in Postgres. `settings` (`key`, `value`, `description`; no policies or grants, read only by the functions) holds the limit for a regular product: `regular_min_days` (3 purchase days) and `regular_max_age_days` (last purchase within 365 days); the admin changes them with an `update` in the SQL Editor. `regular_products(p_user)` returns the product types a household buys by that limit, on the same basis as `purchase_profile` (the column is still called `product_id`; it holds the type id). The link article → type is `article_types` (see "Product types"), without approval beforehand. `offers_for_me()` (`security definer`) returns the currently valid offers that count for you, each once, as JSON with `favorieten` (your favourites that hit an article in it, see "Favourites": by type when the term stands for one, otherwise the term as whole words in the title; the brand exact) and `producten` (regular types with an article in it, through `article_types`); favourites first, then by purchase days. `offers_for_list(p_list)` (`security definer`, guarded by `is_member`) gives per item the stores with a valid offer, through the item's name (`term_articles()`, see "Matching list terms": a type, a brand, a name of a type, or a look-alike) or through `items.offer_id`: the offer an item was put on the list with from Voor jou (set to null when the offer is cleaned up). That link is what gives an offer on the list its label when its title stands for no type, and it shows for every member. The regular-product limit does not apply there. `add_offer_item(p_list, p_offer)` (`security definer`, members only, refuses archived lists) is the only way an offer gets on a list: it inserts a new item named after the offer title (fixed from then on; always a separate row, also when the same product is already on the list) with `offer_id`, `article_supermarket` + `article_id` (only when the offer has exactly one article) and `type_id`: the type when all articles of the offer that have a type share one. In that case, and when the title stood for nothing yet, the title also becomes a name of that type in `term_synonyms` (source `catalog`), so the purchase later counts under the type in the profile. `items.type_id` is a record only: the label runs through the name or the offer. `undo_purchase` does not restore the offer, article or type of such an item (it does for a chosen offer, see "Choosing an offer"); undoing a delete does (`Data.zetItemTerug()`). `offer_details_for_list(p_list)` (same routes, `security definer`) returns per item and offer what exactly is on offer: title, discount text, last valid day and up to 6 article titles with the total, grouped per item in list order and ordered by variant (see "Variant"). The green bar in the header of the list (`bonus-balk`, a button) toggles `bonus-paneel` (`toonBonusPaneel()`), which shows them (`Logica.laadDealDetails()`, `bonusDetailRij()`); it is fetched when the panel opens and again when the labels reload while it is open.

## Variant

Roadmap step 7, part 1. A flat attribute next to the type: one word for the flavour or kind within the type ("tomaat", "kip"), free text, compared on its Dutch stem. It only orders the bonus panel of the list and never excludes anything; there is no list of variants and no screen for them. `article_types` and `term_synonyms` both have `variant`, the generated `variant_stem` (`list_stem`, null when empty) and `variant_judged_at` (set once the AI looked, also when there is no variant to name). The AI gives it in the same call that decides the type: `artikelen-classificeren` per article, `term-classificeren` per term. A row that has a type but no `variant_judged_at` is still waiting: `article_type_work()` offers those articles too and `save_article_types()` then only fills the variant, an article that already has a type keeps it. For names, `term_work(p_limit, p_variants)` adds them to the portion (marked `alleen_variant`) only when asked, which the weekly script does by posting `{ "varianten": true }` to `term-classificeren` until nothing is left; the kick for an unknown term does not, so the label stays as fast as before. `save_term_judgments(p_rows, p_variants)` stores them. `term_variant(p_name)` gives the variant stem of a typed term along the layers of `term_match()`: none for a type name, a bare brand or a tolerant hit, the name's variant otherwise, and for a brand plus a kind the variant of the kind. `offer_details_for_list` puts the offers of one item together, first those with an article of the same variant as the item, and within an offer those articles first; `artikelen_zelfde` says how many, and `artikelenRegel()` shows them bold (`Logica.zelfdeVariant()`).

## Choosing an offer

Roadmap step 7, part 2. In the bonus panel every offer of an item has "Kiezen" (`keuzeBlok()`), also at an item that was put on the list from Voor jou and is the offer itself. It opens the articles of the offer from `offer_choice_options(p_item, p_offer)` (`zelfde`: same variant as the original term, ticked by default; `past`: the term stands for the article; the other articles of the offer only after "Toon ook de n andere artikelen"). "Zet op de lijst" calls `choose_offer(p_item, p_offer, p_articles, p_counts)` (`security definer`, members, valid offers only, at most 20 articles). The choice replaces the item, and every ticked article is its own row: the item itself becomes the first article and each further article is a new item with the same maker and (but for a millisecond) the same `created_at`, so they sit together; nothing ticked is one item named after the offer. Each of them has `items.original_name` (what was typed, "tomatensoep"), `items.offer_choice` (a snapshot `{ titel, korting, geldig_tot, supermarkt, artikelen: [{ artikel_id, titel }] }` with at most one article), `offer_id`, `article_supermarket` + `article_id`, and `choice_group` (the id of the item the choice started with). It is all on the item rows, so Realtime shows it to every member. `itemRow()` keeps such a row as compact as a normal one: the sub line starts with "voor tomatensoep", and the label on the right shows the discount itself ("2 voor 5.99") with "t/m zo" below it, straight from `offer_choice` (`Logica.keuzeVan()`); Every ticked article has a count in the panel (`keuzeAantal()`, minus and plus, 1 to 99; `Logica.keuzeAantal()`, `zetKeuzeAantal()`), sent as `p_counts` (`{ "<article_id>": 2 }`): it becomes the row's `quantity` ("2×", empty for one), and what was there goes to `items.original_quantity`, which comes back with the original name when the choice is cleared or expires. Choosing the offer as a whole leaves the quantity as it was. A chosen item only counts through its offer: `offers_for_list` and `offer_details_for_list` skip the name route when `original_name` is set, and the details carry `gekozen`. "Keuze wissen" (`clear_offer_choice(p_item)`) gives that item its original name back and deletes the other items of the same `choice_group`. An item from Voor jou keeps where it came from in `offer_choice.oorspronkelijke_aanbieding` (set by `choose_offer` when the item had an `offer_id` and no `original_name`): clearing, and expiry, give it that `offer_id` back if the offer still exists, so it is the offer on the list again; a typed item ends without an offer. After the last valid day the same happens by itself: `loadItems()` first calls `reset_expired_choices(p_list)` (`Logica.zetVerlopenKeuzesTerug()`), which per group keeps the oldest item, renamed to the original, for items whose `offer_choice.geldig_tot` has passed or whose offer is gone. `buy_item` stores a chosen item as one purchase with `original_name`, `purchases.article_supermarket` + `article_id` (so `purchase_types()` finds the type without a receipt) and `item_choice` (the item as it was), with which `undo_purchase` restores it with its choice. `save_receipt` stores `original_name` on the purchase and names it after the receipt line when the offer was chosen as a whole; `match_receipt_items` also counts the type of the original name. The panel state while choosing is `keuze` in `logica.js` (`openKeuze()`, `zetKeuzeArtikel()`, `keuzeZichtbaar()`, `bevestigKeuze()`, `wisKeuze()`).

## Dismissing an offer

Roadmap step 7, part 3. "Niet deze week" next to "Kiezen" in the bonus panel hides that offer at that item for every member of the list until the offer expires; an offer is a row in `offers` per week, so the same promotion coming back later is a new row and shows again. `offer_dismissals` (`offer_id`, `item_id`, `list_id`, `user_id`, `dismissed`; both foreign keys cascade) holds it: with an item it counts for the list and `user_id` is who did it, without an item it is personal (Voor jou). Members can select (policy through `is_member(list_id)`, or your own personal rows) and the table is in the realtime publication; writing goes through `dismiss_offer(p_item, p_offer)`, `restore_offer(p_item, p_offer)` and `dismiss_offer_for_me(p_offer)`. Restoring sets `dismissed` to false instead of deleting the row, because Realtime can filter an UPDATE on `list_id` and not a DELETE; `Data.volgLijst()` listens to INSERT and UPDATE and the handler `weggeklikt` in `subscribe()` calls `loadDeals()`. `offers_for_list` returns `weggeklikt` next to `aantal` (which does not count dismissed offers), so an item whose offers are all dismissed loses its Bonus label (`Logica.laadDeals()` keeps only rows with `aantal` above 0); the green bar stays as long as something is dismissed (`Logica.aantalWeggeklikt()`, "1 aanbieding weggeklikt"). `offer_details_for_list` carries `weggeklikt_door`; `renderBonusDetails()` leaves those rows out of the list and shows them in the collapsed block "Weggeklikt (n)" at the bottom of the panel (`wegRij()`: "weggeklikt door Els" and "Terughalen"). Dismissing only applies to the name route: an offer that is on the list itself (`items.offer_id`, from Voor jou or chosen) has no button (`Logica.kanWegklikken()`). In Voor jou every offer has "Niet deze week" too (`klikWegVoorMij()`, `Logica.klikWegVoorMij()`): `offers_for_me()` is a thin wrapper that leaves your own dismissed offers out of `offers_for_me_all()` (service role only). There is no screen to restore those.

## Logging offer choices

Roadmap step 7, part 4. `offer_actions` gets a row for every choice around an offer: `action` (`gekozen`, `gewist`, `weggeklikt`, `teruggehaald`), `list_id` (null for a dismissal in Voor jou), `user_id`, `term` (what was originally on the list), `type_id`, `variant` (the stem from `term_variant()`), the offer as a snapshot (`offer_id` without a foreign key, `supermarket`, `offer_source_id`, `offer_title`, `discount_text`; `offers` is cleaned up after 28 days) and `articles` (the chosen ones). Nothing reads it yet: it is there to improve matching and order later. No policies or grants. The app does nothing for it: `log_offer_action()` is called by `choose_offer()` (a wrapper around `choose_offer_internal()`), `clear_offer_choice()`, `dismiss_offer()`, `restore_offer()` (only when something was actually restored) and `dismiss_offer_for_me()`, and an error in it only raises a warning. An item falling back after the offer expired is not logged. Rows go away with the list; a retention period still has to be set before the public phase.
